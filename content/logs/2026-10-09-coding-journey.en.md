Looking back, every milestone on this path felt accidental at the time, yet inevitable in hindsight.

## Starting Point: CSP

In 2022, I took CCF's CSP-JS certifications and earned Third Prize in both Entry and Advanced levels. Not dazzling scores, but it was the first time I **trained algorithms and programming systematically toward a single goal** — drilling problems, reviewing mistakes, and managing my mindset in the exam hall.

> The value of a certification lies not in the certificate itself, but in how it forces you to distinguish between "writing code" and "solving problems."

## Halfway: A Teaching Trip

In August 2023, I went to Shangri-La for a 50-hour teaching trip, covering world citizenship, art, business and entrepreneurship with local children. Writing lesson plans until dawn, I realised: **explaining what you know to someone else is the most rigorous test of understanding**.

That trip also earned me 6 credits in the ASDAN Volunteering Short Course.

## Now: Turning Ideas into Products

After entering the Software Engineering programme at Shenyang University of Technology, the rhythm changed — no longer just problem drills, but building complete things:

1. **MacIsland**: a macOS Dynamic Island app, the native macOS member of the open-source Pyisland ecosystem. SwiftUI for morph animations, AppKit for windows, GitHub OAuth for the wallpaper community. Currently at v2.5.1.
2. **Light Guard**: a retrofit driver-assistance system for two-wheelers — cameras with dynamic calibration for distance estimation, graduated audio/visual and vibration alerts, and Event Data Recording (EDR) to preserve evidence for liability determination. This project won Third Prize at the 2026 Challenge Cup provincial round.

## Some Reflections

- **Competitions are a means, not an end**: CSP taught me to solve problems; the Challenge Cup taught me to articulate them
- **Writing things down pays off** — hence this blog
- **Ecosystems beat single points**: one Dynamic Island, three Windows implementations (Python/Electron/Tauri), and I filled in macOS — collaboration is far more fun than going it alone

Next: refine what I have, and keep writing.
